### `address-elder`

**Q:** How should I address someone a little older than me?

In Thai, a few years of age difference changes how you speak. Age is a ranking, so you show respect to anyone even slightly older.

**The word to know: พี่ (pîi)**
- Use **pîi** (พี่) = "older sibling." Thai people use it for almost any slightly older person, even a stranger or coworker.
- Attach it to their name: พี่แนน (pîi nɛɛn) = "Older sibling Nan."
- Call yourself **nɔ́ɔŋ** (น้อง) = "younger sibling," or use ผม (phǒm → pǒm) if you are male and ฉัน/ดิฉัน (chǎn / dì-chǎn) if you are female.

**In a real conversation:**
- สวัสดีครับ พี่แนน (sà-wàt-dii kráp, pîi nɛɛn) = Hello, Pii Nan. (male speaker)
- สวัสดีค่ะ พี่แนน (sà-wàt-dii kâ, pîi nɛɛn) = Hello, Pii Nan. (female speaker)

Notice that **kráp** and **kâ** stay on every sentence, not just the greeting. That keeps you in the safe, polite range.

**Common mistake:** Using กู/มึง (guu/mʉʉŋ) or dropping kráp/kâ. That level is only for close friends your own age or younger, never someone older.

**Phrase to use today:** ขอบคุณครับ/ค่ะ พี่ (kɔ̀ɔp-kun kráp/kâ, pîi) = Thank you, Pii.

Want to learn the word for someone *much* older, like พี่ vs ลุง/ป้า (lun/bpâa)?
