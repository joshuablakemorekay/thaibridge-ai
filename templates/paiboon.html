⚠️ Tricky Consonants

  • dt - Unaspirated, between English 'd' and 't'. No puff of air.
  • bp - Unaspirated, between English 'b' and 'p'. No puff of air.
  • ng - Can appear at the start of syllables (unusual for English speakers)
  • r - Rolled or trilled, often softened to 'l' in casual speech
  • Final consonants - At the end of a syllable, Thai only has 8 sounds: k, t, p (cut short, not released), n, m, ŋ, and i/o from ย and ว. So ด, ต, ส, ช all end as t, and ล, ร end as n.

🔤 Vowels

Thai has short and long vowel pairs. Length matters - it changes meaning! Short vowels are pronounced as phonic sounds. There is a distinct Thai accent not to be confused with the many English accents!

⚠️ Hard Stop on Short Vowels

When a syllable ends with a short vowel, there's a hard stop - the vowel is cut off sharply, like the break in "uh-oh". For example:

  • gɔ̀! (เกาะ, island) - The vowel stops abruptly
  • à!-rai (อะไร, what) - Hard stop on the first syllable
  • àat-dtà!-maa (อาตมา, a monk's word for "I") - Hard stop on "dtà"

In fast, natural speech a soft (~) syllable can lose its hard stop and flow into the next one - see Stressed & Soft Syllables below.

Note: The (!) symbol is shown here for learning purposes - it's not part of standard ThaiBridge Spelling.

📏 Short vs. Long Vowels

In ThaiBridge Spelling, doubled letters = long vowels:

  • a (short) vs. aa (long)
  • i (short) vs. ii (long)
  • u (short) vs. uu (long)
  • e (short) vs. ee (long)
  • o (short) vs. oo (long)
  • ɛ (short) vs. ɛɛ (long)
  • ɔ (short) vs. ɔɔ (long)
  • ʉ (short) vs. ʉʉ (long)
  • ə (short) vs. əə (long) - The schwa sound!

The combined vowels ia, ʉa and ua are written the same whether short or long.

🎵 Stressed (-) & Soft (~) Syllables

The Paiboon+ system in the Three-Way Dictionary (Becker & Pirazzi) shows stress between syllables:

  • A syllable followed by - is stressed - said clearly, with its full tone.
  • A syllable followed by ~ is soft - said lightly; in natural speech its tone drifts to mid.
  • The last syllable of a word is always stressed.
  • The written tone mark stays on a soft syllable - the softening is heard, not spelled.

มหาวิทยาลัย má~hǎa-wít-tá~yaa-lai (university) - "má" and "tá" are soft, the rest are stressed.

Across ThaiBridge's lessons every syllable is joined with a plain - to keep things simple, so there it only separates syllables and doesn't mark stress. The ~ is taught here and on Vowels & Syllables.
