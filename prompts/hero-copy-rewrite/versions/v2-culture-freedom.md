# v2 — the culture-freedom line

The follow-up asked whether the hero should carry the app's position on
cultural freedom at all:

> "Should we mention any of this - Adopt aspects of another culture if you wish. Or follow no culture at all. The learner remains free to determine how the teachings fit into their life?"

**Three AI wordings were proposed and all three were rejected.**

| Proposed | Why it was rejected |
|---|---|
| "Keep your own culture, take on part of another, or follow none at all" | Thai culture appears only as an unnamed "another", and is grammatically the one option being declined. "Take on" is the verb for a burden. |
| "practise it however your own life allows" | *"life shoudn't allow life is what we make it"* — writes the reader's life as walls to fit around |
| "learn how the Dhamma is lived in Thailand" | *"it sounds envious"* — puts the reader outside a window watching someone else's practice |

**The failure this version records.** The wording that shipped from this round
promised freedom in general terms and silently dropped the culturally-neutral
option from the front page. The objection had been to the *phrase* "follow no
culture at all" — which contradicts the site's own argument that no Buddhism
sits anywhere culture-free — not to the option. A wording objection was allowed
to delete a commitment, and it took a direct question to catch it:

> "Because this is very important."
