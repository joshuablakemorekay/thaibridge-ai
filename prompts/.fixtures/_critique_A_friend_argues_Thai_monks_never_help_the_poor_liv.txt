As a product, this criticism costs you nothing. As a use of your week, it has
already cost two rounds of edits — and that is the finding worth acting on.

Sorting the criticism. The corruption point is real and well documented:
Thailand has had serious temple scandals and Thais complain about them loudly,
so it is not a hidden truth. The charity claim is wrong. Temples run hospices,
food distribution and flood shelters, and for centuries the village temple was
the only school a poor rural boy could reach. It does not look like a church
soup kitchen because the monastic rules forbid the mechanics — a monk may not
handle money, store food, cook, or eat after midday — so the work runs through
lay committees instead. The history is half right: Theravada reached Thailand
mainly through Sri Lanka rather than directly from India.

What it means for the product. The critic is not in the paying segment. He is
not learning Thai, not visiting temples and not buying. Your paying customer is
the opposite person: someone with a Thai partner, in-laws they cannot speak to,
a visa run, a temple invitation they do not want to get wrong. For them the Thai
cultural material is the product and the only thing separating it from Duolingo.
Sanding it down to satisfy someone outside the funnel would remove the
differentiator and gain nothing.

The one real signal. Your Buddhist content presents the tradition only at its
best, which to a sceptical adult reads as devotional material rather than
teaching. That is a credibility gap and it is cheap to close.

What I would actually do — one section, not a project. Add a short block to an
existing page, /theravada, on how the tradition judges itself: the Vinaya sets
the standard, monks may not handle money or store food, and when Thais are angry
about a jet-owning abbot they are using Buddhism's own rule book to say so. Not
a new route, not a new nav entry. It is real teaching content, it explains the
shape of Thai monastic charity, and it answers the "this is a brochure" charge
without ever mentioning the objection. Roughly an hour of work.

What I would not do. Do not add a page criticising the Sangha — that is a
different product and it earns nothing. Do not touch the free tier: free Dhamma
is both the ethical core and the top of the funnel. And do not write another
defensive paragraph; first-screen space is conversion space.
