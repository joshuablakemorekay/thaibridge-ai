TITLE_THAI: บทพิจารณาสังขาร
TITLE_ENGLISH: The Reflection on Conditioned Things
SOURCE: Composite. Verses 1–3 correspond to Dhammapada 277–279; verses 10–13 to
Dhammapada 41; verses 4–9 are the death-recollection formulas.
WHEN_CHANTED: At morning chanting and again before sleeping, as the book's own
heading directs.

SUMMARY: Thirteen short reflections on impermanence, unsatisfactoriness and
not-self, on the certainty of death, and on the body as a discarded log.

BACKGROUND:
This passage is not a single discourse. It is a reflection compiled by Thai
monastic editors from several places in the Pali tradition and arranged for
daily recitation: the three verses on the characteristics of existence, the
standard formulas for the recollection of death, and the Buddha's verse on the
body abandoned upon the ground.

The Buddha spoke the verse on the body after seeing a corpse cast aside,
unclaimed and of no further use to anyone.

MEANING:
The chant moves inward in three stages. It begins with what is true of all
conditioned things, then narrows to the chanter: life is not lasting, death is
lasting, and death is certain for me.

It closes with the body itself, the thing most easily mistaken for one's own.
The reflection does not ask the practitioner to despise the body but to see
plainly what will become of it, so that clinging loosens of its own accord.
