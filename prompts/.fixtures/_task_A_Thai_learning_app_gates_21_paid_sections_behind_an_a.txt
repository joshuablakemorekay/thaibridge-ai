Yes, the Alphabet is right to be free, and it is not really a generosity call.

All 21 paid sections carry requires_alphabet: True. Every single one. So
charging for the Alphabet would put a paid prerequisite in front of everything
paid: nothing on the language side could be sampled before paying, and the free
tier would collapse to Buddhism only. It is structurally forced.

The harder question is whether the prerequisite is drawn in the right place, and
21 of 21 is a blunt instrument.

Three sections genuinely need the script: vowels_syllables and read_write ARE
the script, and tones_classes derives its tone rules from consonant class, so
the rules cannot be applied without knowing the letters.

Three are not real sections: premium is the pricing page and carries no
@require_access, so its flag never fires; greetings_wai and classifiers have no
route and no template at all.

That leaves 15 sections gated on the alphabet but taught entirely in
romanisation, with Paiboon beside every Thai word. Measured rather than
asserted: CULTURAL_INFO is 31% of strings containing any Thai, TOUR_VOCAB 25%,
FORMALITY_LEVELS 26%, GRAMMAR 37%.

Culture is the starkest case — roughly 10,000 characters, about two-thirds
English prose on the wai, temple manners and festivals, and you must identify 35
of 44 consonants to read it.

Gate order sharpens this. The alphabet gate is checked first, before level and
tier, so a subscriber who has paid £9.99 and opens Tour Guide is told "Complete
Thai Alphabet first" — the paywall was not even what stopped them. I am not
proposing changing the Instant Access Pass; that is the deliberate escape hatch
and it is settled.

Smallest change first: delete requires_alphabet from premium, which is dead
config that reads as "pass a quiz to see our prices" and would become true the
moment someone added the decorator. Then free Culture from the gate, keeping its
level 3 and Basic tier so progression and payment are untouched. Leave the other
14 — learn the script early is a defensible teaching position, it just does not
extend to an English page about bowing.
