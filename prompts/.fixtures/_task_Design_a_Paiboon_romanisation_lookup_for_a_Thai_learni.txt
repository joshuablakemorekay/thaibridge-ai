First, what this can be. I searched the codebase: there is no transliterator
here, and every romanisation the app ships was either hand-written or generated
offline by generate_paiboon.py and then reviewed. So there are three options,
and only one is sane.

1. Search the existing corpus — a box over the Paiboon already written and
checked across the Alphabet, lessons, registers and chanting book. Free,
instant, and correct by construction. Finds about 1,200 entries, not all Thai.

2. Live AI romanisation — costs money per lookup and can be subtly wrong. A
beginner cannot tell a hallucinated tone mark from a real one, and that beginner
is exactly who is using this. Getting a tone wrong is not a typo in Thai, it is
a different word. No.

3. A rule-based engine — weeks of work, and Thai orthography has enough
irregulars (silent ห, implicit vowels, ร as a vowel, loanword spellings) that it
would be wrong often and wrong quietly.

Build the first. A miss says "not in the corpus" and points at the AI tutor; it
never guesses.

The hard part is making it findable. Paiboon writes ขอบคุณ as kɔ̀ɔp-kun and
สวัสดี as sà-wàt-dii, and nobody types that. Three folds run in order: exact,
English gloss, then a deliberately blunt one that forgives tone marks, vowel
length, the ee/ii and oo/uu spellings, the kh/ph/th of RTGS road signs, and
Paiboon's own bp/dt digraphs. So "sawatdee", "khop khun", "aroi" and "gai" all
reach the right rows.

Ranking is band-major — an exact match beats a prefix beats a substring,
whichever fold found it — because otherwise an incidental substring outranks the
real word. And a loose substring only counts when nothing precise was found, or
a good result set gets padded with noise.

It belongs on /paiboon rather than a new route: that page already answers "how
is this written?" in principle, and the box answers it for one word.
