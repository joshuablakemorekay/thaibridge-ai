# Reasoning: Paiboon Lookup

## Goal

I wanted a small daily-use utility that kept people in the app between lessons:

> A Paiboon lookup tool — small utility, daily use, keeps people in the app
> between lessons.

I did not specify how it should work, which turned out to matter — the obvious
implementation is the wrong one.

## Iteration history

**v1 — what can this actually be?** Rather than an implementation, I asked what
the options were. Three came back:

1. **Search the corpus we already have** — the romanisation already written and
   reviewed across the Alphabet, lessons, registers and chanting book. Free,
   instant, correct by construction. Finds about 1,200 entries, not all Thai.
2. **Live AI romanisation** — costs money per lookup and can be subtly wrong.
3. **A rule-based engine** — weeks of work, and Thai orthography has enough
   irregulars that it would be wrong often and wrong quietly.

**v2 — closing it down to one.** I picked the first and shut the door on the
others:

> Yes only do the first one leave the others out of it.

The reason option 2 is disqualified is worth keeping: the person reaching for a
romanisation lookup is a beginner, and a beginner cannot tell a hallucinated
tone mark from a correct one. Getting a tone wrong in Thai is not a typo, it is
a different word.

## Failure modes the final version handles

- **Confident wrong answers.** There is no generation step at all, so there is
  nothing to hallucinate. A miss says "not in the corpus" and points at the AI
  tutor.
- **The spellings nobody types.** Paiboon writes ขอบคุณ as `kɔ̀ɔp-kun` and
  สวัสดี as `sà-wàt-dii`. Three folds run in order — exact, English gloss, then
  a deliberately blunt one forgiving tone marks, vowel length, the ee/ii and
  oo/uu spellings, the kh/ph/th of Thai road signs, and Paiboon's own bp/dt
  notation.
- **An incidental substring outranking a real match.** Searching "chang"
  returned three long sentences above ช้าง itself, because "dì-chǎn gam-laŋ …"
  folds to a string containing "chang" across a syllable boundary. Ranking is
  band-major now — exact beats prefix beats substring, whichever fold found it.
- **A good result set padded with fuzzy noise.** "aroi" returned five matches,
  two of them long price sentences, because `rɔ́ɔi` (hundred) sits inside them.
  A loose *substring* now only counts when nothing precise was found anywhere.
- **The corpus disagreeing with itself.** ช้าง is written both `cháaŋ` and
  `cháang` in different sections. De-duplication is on the folded romanisation,
  so those collapse to one row rather than showing the same word twice.

## Outcome

Shipped in `e368522`, live at `/paiboon`. 1,217 entries drawn from nineteen
sources — Alphabet, Grammar, Vocabulary, Monk Mode, registers, Sentences,
Conversations, the chanting book and more. 34 tests.

Two design choices carried the weight. The corpus is gathered by **walking the
modules** for any dict with both a `thai` and a `paiboon` key, rather than
enumerating the structures by hand — so new content joins the lookup on its own.
That paid off the same day: the Survival Thai section built later in the session
appeared in the lookup with no code change.

And the folded forms are **precomputed at index-build time**. They were
properties in the first cut, which quietly meant folding the whole corpus, three
ways, on every keystroke.

The tool deliberately does not cover all of Thai. The `YAITRON_ENTRIES`
dictionary has 4,894 Thai/Paiboon pairs and was left out on purpose: it *is* the
Pro `/dictionary` feature, and its romanisation was generated by
`generate_paiboon.py` rather than checked by a person — so it fails the standard
the rest of the index meets.

## Engineering on the output

- *Accepted as-is:* the module, the folding rules, the ranking and the tests.
- *Reworked, and why:* nothing at the code level this session.
- *Decisions made:* choose corpus search over live AI and over a rule-based
  engine; exclude the Yaitron dictionary rather than give away the Pro feature
  with unreviewed romanisation; put the box on `/paiboon` rather than a new
  route.
- *Roughly:* shipped as-is on code, with the direction and the product
  decisions mine.

## What I'd change next

The loose fold over-merges by design — "pèt" (spicy) and "pìt" (wrong) collapse
together — and it is only held back from producing noise by the rule that its
substring matches count last. That rule is right but it is a patch on a blunt
instrument, not a sharp one.

The corpus is also only as good as the drafts in it. The chanting book's
romanisation is still unreviewed in places, and the lookup presents every hit
with the same confidence. A "reviewed / draft" marker per source would be more
honest than the source label alone.
